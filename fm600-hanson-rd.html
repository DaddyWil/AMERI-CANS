Walking distance at peak: the 4 minute rings

Inside the rings

The top row and both break tent rows are inside a ring end to end. The rings overlap, so 4 minutes is the edge of coverage, not the typical walk: scaled from the plan, positions sit about 1,300 ft apart along the rows and 770 ft between rows, which puts the farthest point in the core about 750 ft from the nearest unit (a 3 minute walk) and the average walk at about 1.5 minutes.

The one soft spot: the south halves of DC 5, 6 and 7 are 1,400 ft from the spine, a 5 to 6 minute walk. Your plan has no road along the south of that row. If one gets built, we move the second unit at positions 2, 4 and 6 down there and the worst walk on site drops under 3 minutes.

Positions 7 and 8

Without them: DC 4 west end 1,600 ft, HUB 1,400 ft, CMDA east 2,700 ft. With them: 1,050 ft, 500 ft and 1,050 ft.

Stop 4 of 10

G1: main entrance checkpoint, 4 stall trailer

Who uses it

Guards and flaggers who work this post all shift, and drivers queued in the relief and truck staging area, which runs 1,100 ft south to Hanson Rd. Today that queue has nowhere to go.

Why a trailer, not a portable

This is the first thing every visitor and inspector sees. The 4 stall is a climate controlled trailer: four private stalls, each with a flushing toilet, sink, paper towels and soap. It reads like the front door of a serious site.

Where and what it needs

West shoulder at the check point, parallel to the road, so the service truck stops without blocking the lane. A level 25 x 10 ft spot (23 ft trailer with the tongue); 3 x 30 A/120 V (or 3 x 15 A, or 1 x 50 A/240 V) power; truck within 10 ft of the door side. 200 gal fresh on board or city fill, 440 gal waste. Weekly service at this traffic, more if the queue is long.

If the guard post grows: swap in the 8 stall trailer (fleet table in the appendix). Same pad logic, 35 ft long.

The OSHA check

29 CFR 1926.51 Table D-1: over 200 workers, 1 toilet seat and 1 urinal per 50. At 2,400 that is 48 seats and 48 urinals. Twelve MARK IVs carry 120 seats and 96 urinals. Comfortably over the floor.

The surge lever

If headcount runs past the plan, we move the busiest units to twice daily service, same pads, same day: 388 to 456 people per unit. No new pad, no new power drop.

Stop 8 of 10

We also plumb office trailers

Not part of the restroom count above. If any office trailer on this site needs running water and a working restroom, this is how we do it: a pump house for supply, insulated waste tanks under the trailer, and the same truck that services the MARK IVs keeps both topped up and pumped out.

1Fill connection outside the pump house: the truck tops up the 500 gal tank on the service cadence. Overflow outlet beside it.
2Pump house: insulated 4 x 8 ft shed with a 200 gal or 500 gal poly tank, two delivery pumps and a float switch so the pumps never run dry.
3Supply line to the trailer, wrapped in insulation with heat trace underneath. Needs power 24/7 in winter.
4Waste leaves the trailer by gravity through a 3 in PVC Sch 40 drain line.
5Insulated poly waste tank, 300 gal, or two 300 gal tanks for a large trailer. The tanks fit under the trailer. Cleanout the truck pumps from.
6Power: two independent dedicated 120 V circuits at the pump house, one for the pumps and one for the heat trace.
Sizing rule of thumb: 16 to 20 people in a trailer use about 900 to 1,100 gal of water a week and make 1,000 to 1,200 gal of waste. That sets the tank size and how often we come.
Larger trailer systems: multi-trailer complexes and high headcount offices are accommodated with more robust, custom built options, sized to the complex and set at the site walk.

500 gal pump house: field trailer water and waste system

DESCRIPTION

Total functional areas: one insulated pump house per customer trailer, feeding the trailer's own sinks and toilets; insulated waste holding tanks under the trailer. Larger trailer systems are accommodated with more robust, custom built options.

Water side: 200 gal or 500 gal poly tank, two delivery pumps with a float switch (prevents pump burnout), fill connection and overflow outlet outside the shed, supply line insulated with heat trace underneath.

Waste side: 3 in PVC Sch 40 gravity drain line from the trailer to one or two 300 gal insulated poly waste tanks, set under the trailer, each with a cleanout for truck pumping.

AMERI-CANS build notes: insulated 4 x 8 ft shed, winterized; pumps and float switch included; maintenance, water refill, waste pumping and weatherproofing included in the service cadence.

CRITICAL SPECIFICATIONS

Power requirements: two independent, dedicated 120 V circuits (pump operation; heat trace). Continuous 24/7 supply in winter.

Fresh water capacity: 200 gal or 500 gal (950 gal pump houses also available). Waste capacity: 300 gal per tank, one or two tanks under the trailer.

Footprint: 4 ft x 8 ft shed beside the trailer; the waste tanks sit under the trailer. Truck access within 20 ft of the fill point and the cleanout.

Planning reference: 16 to 20 personnel use about 885 to 1,106 gal of water a week and produce 974 to 1,216 gal of wastewater. Installation fee set after the site assessment.
